11. Visualizing Average Bond and Stock Ratios with Stacked Bars

ratio_summary <- longdata |>
  filter(name %in% c("bondRatio", "stockRatio")) |>
  pivot_wider(names_from = name, values_from = value) |>
  group_by(YEAR, COMPANY_TYPE_DESC) |>
  summarise(
    bondRatio = mean(bondRatio, na.rm = TRUE),
    stockRatio = mean(stockRatio, na.rm = TRUE),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>
  pivot_longer(
    cols = c(bondRatio, stockRatio),
    names_to = "name",
    values_to = "value"
  )

ratio_summary |>
  ggplot(aes(x = YEAR, y = value, fill = name)) +
  geom_col() +
  facet_wrap(~COMPANY_TYPE_DESC) +
  ylim(c(0, 1)) +
  labs(
    title = "Average Bond and Stock Ratios by Company Type",
    x = "Year",
    y = "Average Ratio",
    fill = "Ratio"
  ) +
  theme(
    axis.text.x = element_text(angle = 45, hjust = 1),
    legend.position = "bottom"
  )

12. Brief Description of Findings

The visualizations show that total assets generally increased over time, although there is substantial variation across insurers and several very large outliers. The median total assets rose from about $16.3 million in 1989 to about $346.4 million in 2022. Bonds make up a much larger share of assets than common stocks overall: the average bond ratio is about 55.4%, compared with about 5.6% for the stock ratio. Mutual companies have a somewhat higher average bond ratio than stock companies, while the average stock ratios are fairly similar. The focused box plots also make the typical distributions easier to see because the extreme asset values otherwise compress most observations near the bottom of the graph.
